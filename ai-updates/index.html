Anthropic · 8 October 2026

Anthropic clarifies its Usage Policy for more autonomous Claude work.

Newest verified update
ANTHROPIC · USAGE POLICYOfficial update · 8 Oct

Anthropic’s annual policy update makes several existing rules easier to apply to longer-running AI work. The revised policy takes effect on 12 November 2026.

In simple terms

Think of this as a clearer rulebook for using Claude. Anthropic says most changes explain rules it already enforced, including safeguards around deceptive campaigns, elections, high-risk advice, physical actions, and model abuse.

Technical view

The revision consolidates bans on fake accounts and concealed influence operations; narrows election restrictions around deception and disruption; clarifies surveillance and weapon-enabling software limits; and adds operator controls for hardware that can take harmful physical actions. Human review and disclosure requirements for high-risk health, legal, financial, livelihood, and essential-service recommendations remain in place.

Who it matters to: Teams publishing campaigns or building agents, and organizations using Claude in health, finance, law, public services, surveillance, or physical systems.

Availability and limits: Effective 12 November 2026. Anthropic describes most provisions as clarification of existing enforcement. The high-risk human-review and disclosure requirements are not new. Review the full policy for your use case; this summary is not legal advice.

Anthropic · 7 October 2026

Claude Haiku 5.5 targets fast, high-volume work at lower cost.

Verified update
ANTHROPIC · MODEL + PRICINGOfficial update · 7 Oct

Anthropic introduced Haiku 5.5 for quick, repeated tasks such as summaries, classification, support, browser use, and subagent work. It also cut Claude Sonnet 5.5 cache-read prices.

In simple terms

Haiku is the smaller, faster option for lots of short jobs. Anthropic says it costs around 75% less per task on average than Haiku 4.5. Larger models may still be better for the hardest work.

Technical view

The API model ID is claude-haiku-5-5. For prompts up to 100K tokens, list prices per million tokens are $0.10 input and $0.50 output; above 100K, $0.50 input and $2.50 output. Anthropic says average task cost is about 75% lower, accounting for a tokenizer change. Sonnet 5.5 cache reads fall from $0.20 to $0.10 per million tokens, which Anthropic estimates makes most agentic work about 20% cheaper. Claude Python and TypeScript SDK computer/browser use is in beta.

Who it matters to: Developers and operations teams processing many narrow, repeatable tasks, and Max or Team subscribers exploring the API credit offer.

Availability and limits: Available on Claude and major cloud platforms. Max 5x subscribers get $100 monthly API credit, Max 20x $200, and Team up to $500 pooled, according to Anthropic. Credits are for Claude Platform API use. Anthropic says Sonnet and Opus remain better for complex agentic coding; compare your own task quality, token use, latency, and review cost.

Anthropic expands verified access for cybersecurity teams.

Verified update
ANTHROPIC · VERIFIED CYBER ACCESSOfficial update · 6 Oct

Anthropic combined its cyber access programs into three verification tiers, giving qualified security organizations different levels of access based on the work they are authorized to do.

In simple terms

The most capable cyber features are not open to everyone. Security teams can apply for a tier that fits defensive work, approved red-team testing, or a narrow set of critical-infrastructure tests.

Technical view

The three tiers can provide access to models including Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, and Mythos 5.1. Availability and limits: Access requires application, verification, and security controls. Red Team Access is organization-only and only for authorized systems; high-impact destructive actions remain blocked. Program data retention is required, with limited stated zero-data-retention exceptions while Enterprise Frontier Safeguards is developed. Standard Claude models still support defensive code review and known-issue patching.

OpenAI · 5 October 2026

OpenAI begins optional text watermarking and a limited detector pilot.

Verified update
OPENAI · TEXT PROVENANCEOfficial update · 5 Oct

OpenAI described an invisible statistical signal for selected model text outputs, with API opt-in available globally and a planned EU rollout for eligible ChatGPT and Codex text.

In simple terms

A watermark may help identify that an OpenAI model contributed to some text. It cannot identify the person who wrote it, measure how much a human edited it, or tell whether it is true.

Technical view

OpenAI’s textGrain approach adds a statistical signal to token choices. API customers can opt in for select models; it remains off by default. OpenAI plans to add invisible watermarks to eligible ChatGPT and Codex text in the EU over the coming weeks. Detector access starts with approved researchers and expert organizations because short, constrained, edited, or translated text may be missed and false positives remain possible.

Who it matters to: API developers, publishers, educators, researchers, and teams that need to understand provenance signals or disclosure practices.

Availability and limits: At the time of the announcement, the API option is global but opt-in; ChatGPT and Codex rollout is planned for eligible EU text only. The detector is restricted to approved organizations. A detected or absent watermark does not prove authorship, ownership, accuracy, or the amount of human contribution.
