About/Editorial standards
How we work

Clarity, context, and trust.

These standards guide how LeadGenLab plans, writes, reviews, and updates its stories.

Accuracy and sources

We aim to verify material claims with original documents, product documentation, research, or direct statements. Stories should distinguish reported facts from interpretation and link readers to useful sources.

Benchmarks and practical tests

We label benchmarks published by a product maker as company-reported results and link to the original source. When LeadGenLab runs a hands-on test, we describe the task, model or feature, date, and evaluation criteria so readers can understand what was tested. We do not present a planned, simulated, or reader-led exercise as a LeadGenLab test. If we have not independently tested a result, we say so.

Plain language

We explain technical terms the first time we use them and avoid overstating what a tool or development can do. Our goal is understanding, not hype.

Product coverage and conflicts

Product reviews and guides should state the criteria used. Any sponsorship, affiliate relationship, or relevant commercial relationship will be disclosed near the coverage. Commercial relationships do not buy favorable editorial treatment.

Corrections and updates

If we find a significant error, we will correct it and make the change clear in the article. Time-sensitive pages should show when they were last reviewed.

AI-assisted work

AI tools may support research or drafting, but a human editor remains responsible for checking claims, sources, and the final published text.
